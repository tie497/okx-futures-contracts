## What does “futures OKX” actually refer to?

On OKX, the term “futures” can refer to several related derivatives products:

- **Expiry futures**, which have a defined settlement or expiration date.
- **Perpetual futures**, which do not have a fixed expiration date.
- **Crypto-margined futures**, where the underlying cryptocurrency is used as collateral and settlement currency.
- **USDT-margined futures**, where USDT is used for margin and profit-and-loss settlement.
- **USDC-margined or USDⓈ-margined futures**, available only in supported markets and jurisdictions.

Perpetual futures are often the product people mean when they search for OKX futures. They track the price of an underlying asset but do not expire. Instead, a funding mechanism helps keep the perpetual contract price near the underlying market price. Funding is exchanged between long and short traders and is separate from the trading fee.

The most important decision is usually not “long or short?” It is **which margin and settlement structure matches your account**.

## OKX futures contract types compared

### USDT-margined futures

USDT-margined futures use USDT as collateral and settle profits and losses in USDT. This makes the result easier to track in a relatively stable unit than a crypto-margined contract.

For example, a BTCUSDT perpetual contract generally uses:

- BTC/USDT as the reference market
- USDT as collateral
- USDT as the profit-and-loss settlement currency
- A contract size defined in the underlying asset, such as 0.01 BTC per contract for the example shown in OKX documentation
- 24/7 trading, subject to product availability and platform rules

This structure is often easier for traders who want their futures account balance to remain denominated in USDT rather than in BTC or another volatile asset.

### Crypto-margined futures

Crypto-margined futures use the underlying cryptocurrency as collateral and settlement currency. A BTCUSD perpetual position, for example, may use BTC as margin and settle profit or loss in BTC.

That creates an additional source of exposure. Your position can move according to the BTC contract price, while the value of your BTC collateral also changes against the currency you use to measure your portfolio.

Crypto-margined futures may be useful for a trader who already holds the underlying asset and wants to hedge or maintain crypto-denominated exposure. They are less straightforward if you want to measure every result in stablecoin terms.

### USDⓈ-margined futures

USDⓈ-margined products can use settlement currencies such as USDC, USDG, or USD where supported. Availability is not universal. The actual settlement currency and leverage limit depend on the contract and the jurisdiction attached to your account.

Do not assume that seeing a product in an article means it will appear in your trading interface. OKX states that product availability and fee rules can differ by location, account type, and regulatory requirements.

### Expiry futures versus perpetual futures

Expiry futures have a scheduled settlement date. Perpetual futures do not have a fixed expiration date, but they use funding payments to help keep the contract aligned with the underlying market.

That distinction changes how you plan a position:

## Funding fees are separate from trading fees

Perpetual futures do not expire, so they need a mechanism to keep their price close to the spot market. That mechanism is the funding rate.

Depending on the funding rate and whether you are long or short, you may:

- Pay funding to the other side of the market.
- Receive funding from the other side.
- Pay or receive funding at different intervals for different contracts.

The funding interval is not necessarily identical across all pairs. OKX documentation gives BTCUSDT perpetual as an example with an 8-hour funding interval, while other contracts may use different intervals. The trading interface displays the relevant funding information for the selected contract.

A position that remains open for several funding periods can accumulate a meaningful cost even when the trading fee was low. Before opening a perpetual position, check:

1. The current funding rate.
2. The next funding timestamp.
3. Whether the displayed rate is positive or negative.
4. The estimated funding amount for your position size.
5. Whether the contract uses an unusual funding interval.

A low entry fee does not automatically mean the position is inexpensive to hold.

## Leverage, margin, and liquidation

Leverage lets you control a larger notional position with less initial margin. It also reduces the distance between your entry price and a liquidation event.

OKX supports cross-margin and isolated-margin modes for futures. In isolated margin, a specified amount of margin is assigned to a particular position. In cross margin, available margin can be shared across positions according to the account and risk model.

### Isolated margin

Isolated margin is easier to contain at the position level. If the trade moves against you, the margin assigned to that position is the main amount at risk under the relevant liquidation rules.

This does not make the position safe. A highly leveraged isolated position can still be liquidated quickly. It simply separates the position’s margin from funds allocated elsewhere.

### Cross margin

Cross margin can use a broader portion of the available margin balance to support open positions. That may give a position more room before liquidation, but it can also expose a larger part of the account to a losing trade.

For traders still learning how futures work, confusing cross margin with a protective stop-loss is a serious mistake. Cross margin changes how collateral is shared. It does not limit the market loss.

### Why liquidation can happen before the chart reaches your expected level

Liquidation is based on the exchange’s margin and risk calculations, including factors such as:

- Mark price rather than only the last traded price.
- Maintenance margin requirements.
- Position size.
- Leverage.
- Other open positions.
- Account mode.
- Unrealized profit and loss.
- Funding payments and trading costs.
- Contract-specific risk limits.

OKX’s margin documentation explains that initial margin and maintenance requirements vary according to the contract type, leverage, position size, and margin mode.

A position can therefore be liquidated even if the latest traded price appears not to have touched the level you were watching. The relevant liquidation calculation may use the mark price and account-level margin conditions.

## A practical way to choose an OKX futures contract

The decision can be reduced to four questions.

### 1. What currency do you want to use as collateral?

If you want profit and loss measured in USDT, start by examining USDT-margined contracts.

If you hold BTC or another cryptocurrency and intentionally want crypto-denominated exposure, crypto-margined futures may be more suitable. Remember that the collateral itself can move in value.

### 2. Do you need a perpetual contract?

Perpetual futures are flexible because they do not have a fixed expiration date. They also introduce funding payments, which can become a recurring cost.

Expiry futures may be easier to plan around when you have a defined trading horizon, but you must understand the settlement date and contract rules.

### 3. Are you trading directionally or hedging?

A directional trader opens a long or short position to benefit from a price move.

A hedge may use futures to offset exposure in a spot portfolio. For example, someone holding a cryptocurrency may use a short futures position to reduce downside exposure during a particular period. The hedge will not be perfect if the contract, size, timing, or settlement currency does not match the underlying holdings.

### 4. What is the maximum loss you are prepared to accept?

This should be answered before choosing leverage. Starting with the highest leverage displayed by the interface reverses the order of the decision.

A more disciplined sequence is:

1. Decide the maximum account amount you are willing to risk.
2. Choose isolated or cross margin.
3. Select the contract and settlement currency.
4. Calculate the position’s notional value.
5. Set leverage conservatively.
6. Place a stop-loss or other exit rule.
7. Check the estimated liquidation price.
8. Review fees and funding before confirming the order.

## What fees should you include in your calculation?

Before opening a position, estimate more than the visible trading commission.

The main costs and account risks can include:

- Maker or taker trading fee.
- Funding payment for perpetual futures.
- Forced liquidation fee if the position is liquidated.
- Expiry settlement fee for applicable expiry products.
- Deposit, withdrawal, conversion, or payment-provider charges.
- Slippage between the displayed price and executed average price.
- Spread between available bids and asks.

OKX states that futures may involve funding fees, liquidation fees charged at the current taker rate, and an expiry settlement fee of 0.01% for applicable products.

A useful pre-trade calculation is:

text
Estimated round-trip trading cost
= entry notional value × entry fee rate
+ exit notional value × exit fee rate
+ expected funding payments
+ expected slippage


This is only an estimate. The final amount depends on actual fills, execution type, price changes, and the fee schedule applied to your account.

## Is OKX futures suitable for beginners?

OKX provides a broad derivatives interface, but that does not make futures beginner-friendly by default. The most difficult part is usually not finding the long and short buttons. It is understanding how position size, collateral, leverage, funding, and liquidation interact.

A beginner should consider starting with:

- A small position size.
- Isolated margin.
- Low leverage.
- One liquid contract.
- A pre-defined stop-loss.
- No strategy that depends on constantly adding margin.
- A written maximum loss for each trade.

Avoid using the full available balance simply because the platform allows it. Available margin is not the same as sensible risk capacity.

Perpetual futures can be useful for directional trading or hedging, but they are unsuitable for money you cannot afford to lose. The platform’s own risk disclosures emphasize that leveraged derivatives can magnify both gains and losses and may be subject to automatic liquidation.
